# The Smallest Thing

**From the episode:** *The certification answers the wrong question.*

You cannot choose a road until you have chosen somewhere to go. This page gives you
the destination — one small, real job you could be trusted with — and then the
study question answers itself.

- **What it is:** one job you could own, and the short list of what would earn it.
- **Who fills it in:** you, alone. Nobody has to see this.
- **When:** tonight, before you open another comparison.
- **Time:** about thirty minutes. Most of it is step 2, and that is the work.

## How to fill it in

1. **Write down three things you have seen go wrong** — at work, on a course, in
   something you built, in a story somebody told you. Not disasters. Small
   annoyances: the thing that keeps breaking, the job nobody wants, the request that
   always takes three days.
2. **Pick the one you would not mind being called about.** That is the test, and it
   is the only one. Not the most impressive, not the best paid — the one where
   being the person who gets called sounds more interesting than annoying.
3. **Write it as a job, not a title.** "Whoever keeps the backups working." "The
   person who runs the access reviews." "Whoever gets called when the nightly job
   fails." **If your sentence contains a job title, it is not small enough yet** —
   nobody hands out titles, they hand out jobs.
4. **Now write what someone would need to see to trust you with it.** Three or four
   things, maximum, and each one has to be something you could show or say. "Has
   done it once" counts. "Understands it" does not, because it cannot be shown.
5. **Mark each line: have it / do not have it / do not know.** **"Do not know" is a
   legal answer** and it is usually the most useful line on the page, because it
   tells you what to go and find out rather than what to go and study.
6. **Then, and only then, ask your study question.** With this page in front of you
   it takes about ten minutes and you will not need anyone's opinion.

## If nothing on your list feels small enough

Cut it in half. Then cut it again. "Cloud security" is a field. "Cloud security for
one application" is a job. "The access reviews for one team's cloud accounts" is a
thing somebody would genuinely hand to a person next week.

The smaller it gets, the more real it gets, and the easier every other decision
becomes.

## Rules that keep it honest

1. **A job, never a title.** The whole page fails on this one line. If you can put
   it on a business card, it is too big.
2. **"Do not know" beats a guess, every time.** It converts a study problem into a
   question you can ask a real person, which is a much better position to be in.
3. **Do not write the certification down on this page.** Not in any box. Answer the
   destination first; the road is a ten-minute question afterwards and it will not
   feel like a decision at all.

## What this buys you

A destination, which is the thing every study question has been quietly waiting
for. Choosing what to learn stops being a research project and becomes obvious.
And the line at the bottom — the first thing to go and find out — is something you
can act on tonight, which is more than another comparison was ever going to give
you.
