# Month Seven

**From the episode:** *The roadmap is not the hard part.*

Somewhere in the middle of your plan the milestones stop and the silence starts.
This page writes down, while you are still enthusiastic, how you will tell it is
working when nothing is finishing.

- **What it is:** one capability, one way to check it, and a date.
- **Who fills it in:** you, alone. Nobody sees this and nobody grades it.
- **When:** now — early, while it still feels easy. Filling this in at month seven
  is like designing a fire escape during the fire.
- **Time:** about twenty minutes.

## How to fill it in

1. **Write down one thing that beat you recently.** A task you could not finish, an
   error you could not read, a concept that would not go in. Be specific enough
   that you could try it again — "networking" is not it, "could not work out why one part of the app could not reach the database" is.
2. **Write what you will be able to do in a few months that you cannot do today.**
   **It must have a verb in it.** *Do*, not *know*. "Read a stack trace and say
   where to start looking" is checkable. "Understand debugging" is not, and an
   uncheckable line is worse than a blank one because it feels like an answer.
3. **Write how you will check.** The check has to be something you can do on a
   Tuesday evening, alone, for free, and get a clear yes or no from. The easiest
   one is almost always: try the thing from step 1 again.
4. **Pick a date and put it in your calendar now.** Not a reminder to study — a
   reminder to *check*. Three months out is usually right. The date is not
   motivation; it is the event that the middle refuses to supply.
5. **Write what you will do if the answer is no.** Decide it now, calmly, while
   nothing is at stake. "Ask someone" and "give it another eight weeks" are both
   fine. **What is not fine is deciding it on the day**, because on the day you will
   be tired and you will read a no as a verdict on you.
6. **Then close the page and forget it until the date.** Genuinely. It works by
   being out of sight.

## The page

**Something that beat me recently** *(specific enough to try again)*:

> ________________________________________

**By ______________ I will be able to** *(a verb, something I can do)*:

> ________________________________________

**How I will check** *(Tuesday evening, alone, free, clear answer)*:

> ________________________________________

**The date is in my calendar:** ______________  *(tick when it actually is)*

**If the answer is no, I will:**

> ________________________________________

*(example — replace all of it)*

> *Something that beat me: could not work out why one part of the app could not reach the > database. By November I will be able to: work through a connection failure and say
> which layer it is in, without looking up the order. How I will check: rebuild that
> same broken setup and try again, with a timer. If the answer is no: ask the person
> in the study group who does networking, and give it another two months — one no
> is not information yet.*

## When the date arrives

Read only what you wrote. Do the check. Then one of two things is true, and both
are useful:

- **Yes** — you now have evidence, which is the thing the middle stopped giving
  you. Write the next one straight away, while the good feeling is doing the work.
- **No** — that is information about the plan, not about you. Do the thing you
  already decided in step 5. **You are not allowed to invent a new interpretation
  on the day**; that is exactly the moment when a bad month gets promoted into a
  conclusion about your ability.

## Rules that keep it honest

1. **A verb, or it does not count.** *Know*, *understand* and *be comfortable with*
   are all uncheckable, and an uncheckable line gives you nothing on the day.
2. **Decide the "no" answer in advance.** The whole page exists because judgment is
   worse when you are tired and unsure, and month seven is both.
3. **One line, not a plan.** If you find yourself writing five capabilities, you
   have rebuilt the roadmap — and the roadmap is not the part that was failing you.

## What this buys you

A way to tell that it is working when nothing is finishing — which is the only
thing the middle actually takes from you. Progress was never the problem; evidence
was. This page is twenty minutes of your enthusiastic self doing a favour for your
tired self, and it is the cheapest favour available.
