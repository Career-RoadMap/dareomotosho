# The Holiday Test

**From the episode:** *It runs without you when the decision does.*

Your process documents say how the work is done. They cannot say what to do when
the situation is not the one in the document — and that is every situation that
waits for you. This page writes down the missing layer, three lines at a time.

- **What it is:** the three lines from the episode, for one recurring decision —
  with the decision itself at the top and what would change your mind at the bottom.
- **Who fills it in:** you, first. Then the person who would have to make the call.
- **When:** now, for whatever stopped last time you were away. Again whenever a new
  decision starts routing through you.
- **Time:** fifteen minutes for the first one. Five for each after that.

## How to fill it in

1. **Name the thing that actually stopped.** Not a category — the specific thing.
   "The Okafor quote sat for nine days" is a decision. "Sales" is not. If nothing
   comes to mind, ask whoever covered for you; they will remember.
2. **Write what you optimise for, in plain words.** Not the mission statement. The
   real thing you protect when it gets tight — speed, or margin, or never letting
   one particular customer down. One phrase.
3. **Then write what you are willing to lose to get it.** This is the line people
   skip and it is the line that makes the page work. A priority with nothing
   underneath it is a preference, and nobody can act on a preference.
4. **Put a name against the collision.** Who decides when the first two pull
   against each other? A person, not a role and not a committee — the future reader
   needs somebody to ask.
5. **Then give it to that person and ask one question:** would you have made the
   same call? If they say no, you have not lost anything — you have found the
   conversation a document was never going to start.
6. **Then stop.** This is not a policy. Three lines, one decision, and back to work.

## The form — one per decision

**The decision that stopped:**

> ________________________________________

**What we optimise for here:**

> ________________________________________

**What we are willing to lose to get it:**

> ________________________________________

**Who decides when those two collide** *(a name, not a role)*:

> ________________________________________

**What would have to change for us to decide differently:**

> ________________________________________

## Which decision to start with

You do not need many of these, and picking well matters more than doing lots. In
order:

- The one that waited for you last time you were away.
- The one people ask you about in a message that begins "quick one".
- The one where you have overruled somebody in the last month.

If none of those is obvious, the honest answer is **you do not know yet** — write
that down and watch for a fortnight. Guessing produces a page nobody uses.

## Rules that keep it honest

1. **"What we are willing to lose" must name something real.** If it does not
   sting slightly to write, it is not a trade-off, it is a slogan.
2. **A name, never a team.** A decision owned by everyone stops in exactly the way
   this page exists to prevent.
3. **Do not write these for decisions that are not really yours.** If the answer is
   already obvious to the person on the ground, documenting it just adds a page
   between them and the work.

## What this buys you

The next time you are away, the question that used to wait for you gets answered by
somebody else — the same way you would have answered it, for the reason you would
have used. And the first time somebody reads your second line and says "I would not
have given that up", you have found out something about your business that no
process document was ever going to tell you.
