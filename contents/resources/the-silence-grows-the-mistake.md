# The Say-It-Early Note

*From the episode: A Mistake at Work I Regret: Going Silent, and What to Say*

Getting something wrong at work is usually a skill problem, and people can help you with that.
Keeping quiet about it turns it into a trust problem, so this page takes you from "I think
something I did is wrong" to a short, plain message, sent early.

- **What it is:** a one-page note for one thing you think you got wrong at work: whether it
  needs saying, who to tell, the three sentences to say, and the updates until it is closed.
- **Who fills it in:** anybody in their first job or first years in tech, or anybody whose work
  had ChatGPT or Gemini in it and who is no longer sure it was right.
- **When:** as soon as you have a credible concern, which means a real reason to think something
  is wrong. You do not need to be certain first.
- **Time:** about ten minutes for the note. If it is taking longer, you are writing a speech; go
  back to three sentences.

---

## How to fill it in

1. **Write what you noticed, in one line.** What looks wrong, where it is, and how you spotted it.
2. **Write what you have checked, and what you still do not know.** "I don't know yet" is a
   legal answer here. Say it rather than guess.
3. **Answer the three questions.** Tick each one that is a yes. One yes means you say it today.
   If you cannot tell, treat it as a yes.
4. **Name the person who needs to know.** A name, not a team, so you know exactly who to go to.
5. **Write your three sentences.** What happened. What you have already done. What you need from
   them. One sentence each, built from steps 1 and 2.
6. **Write one sorry, and cross out any excuse.** "The AI did it" is an excuse. If ChatGPT or
   Gemini helped, write what you checked and what you did not.
7. **Tell them, then write when and how.** Speak to them directly if you can, then follow it up
   in writing with the same three sentences.
8. **Stay for the clean-up.** Write each update you send, with the day, until it is closed.

## Rules that keep it honest

- **Three sentences, not a speech.** If a sentence is there to defend you, it is not one of the
  three.
- **Sorry once, and mean it.** Then move to the fix. Ten minutes of apologising leaves the problem
  sitting there.
- **Telling them once and disappearing is not ownership.** The note is finished when the
  problem is closed, not when the message is sent.

---

## What this buys you

On the day that feeling arrives, you are not building a defence in your head. You have what you
noticed, who to tell, and three plain sentences, and the mistake stops growing at the size it
was. The line to keep in mind while you fill it in:

> **Say something as soon as you have a credible concern. Explain what you've noticed, what you've checked, and what you still don't know.**
