# The Decision Audit

**From the episode:** *Tokenmaxxing is measuring the input.*

Token spend is an input. Spending more of it is not evidence anything improved — any more than a rising cloud bill was. This audit asks the one question that separates spend from result: what decision changed because the tool was there?

**What it is:** one table, one column per AI initiative.
**Who fills it in:** whoever owns the AI budget, with the initiative leads in the room — the leads have the row-two stories.
**When:** monthly, or before approving any increase in AI spend.
**Time:** about thirty minutes for two or three initiatives.

## How to fill it in

1. **One column per initiative.** Name it the way the invoice names it.
2. **Row one — monthly spend.** From the bill, not from memory.
3. **Row two is the whole audit.** Name one decision your organisation made differently — or faster, or with fewer people — because the tool was there. A real event, with a date and a name. "The pricing review on the 14th used the model's summary and finished in one sitting" is an entry. "Productivity improved" is not. If nobody can name a decision, leave the cell empty — an empty cell is the honest result, and it is what this audit exists to surface.
4. **Rows three to five — follow the trail.** Who made that decision, what deciding the old way would have cost, and who would notice first if the tool was switched off tomorrow.

## How to read what you wrote

- A filled row two means the spend is buying decisions. That is the result.
- An empty row two is not failure — it is an unmeasured initiative. Decide deliberately whether to measure it or stop it.
- "Nobody would notice for a month" in the last row is a legitimate answer, and the most useful sentence this page can produce.
- An initiative with an empty row two for two audits in a row is not unlucky. Either instrument it or admit it is a bet.

## The reframe this protects you from

A usage dashboard makes more input look like progress. A decision log cannot be gamed that way — either the decision happened or it did not.
